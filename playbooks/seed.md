**Answer the unanswered message.** *If any member question is unanswered.* This
outranks everything else in this file. At single-digit membership one ignored
message is a person who won't come back. The action is: reply to that specific
person now.

**Ask one question of one silent member.** *If a member has never posted, or has
gone quiet.* Not a broadcast to the channel — a direct message to a named person
asking one specific thing: what did you try to make, and what went wrong. This is
the highest-information-per-effort action available at this size.

**Merge channels down.** *If more than about three channels exist and most have
no activity in 30 days.* Empty channels split what little attention exists and
make a small server look abandoned. The action is: pick the channels to delete or
archive, by name.

**Post the work, not an announcement.** *If the founder has gone quiet.* A
founder who stops posting is the most common cause of a dead early server, and
"post more" is not actionable. What works is shipping artefacts: something the
product made this week, something it failed at, a before-and-after. The action
names the specific artefact.

**Show a failure.** *If the product is unfinished and the founder is reluctant to
post.* Posting what the product got wrong recruits the people who care about
fixing it, and is easier to write than a launch post. This is a play, not a
platitude — name the specific failure to post.

**Recruit against a specific request.** *If the founder has bandwidth for
outbound.* Someone somewhere has described exactly the thing they can't model.
Fulfil it by hand, publish the result, and the person becomes a user, a
testimonial, and a roadmap item at once. Do things that don't scale; fifty times
by hand before automating any of it.

**Convert a lurker with a gift.** *If a member joined and never spoke.* Make the
thing they'd want and hand it to them unprompted. Costs one evening, converts far
better than any prompt to participate.

**Do nothing to the server.** *If the server is quiet but the product has no
users.* A legitimate and frequently correct recommendation. The bottleneck is
upstream; say so plainly and put the founder's time where it moves the north star.

## Explicitly forbidden at seed stage

Do not recommend these below 25 members, however tempting. Each needs a crowd,
and each reads as failure when it lands in an empty room.

Scheduled events, weekly rituals, office hours, AMAs, tournaments or contests,
XP or level systems, leaderboards, currency or economy mechanics, quests, daily
streaks, role hierarchies, ambassador or mod programmes, onboarding flows with
more than one step, adding channels, welcome bots.

## The Dank Memer layer — later, not now

Economy and progression mechanics genuinely work: progression, status,
leaderboards, daily-return loops, a prestige endgame. They work because a crowd
generates the competition and the audience that make status worth having. They
are a **growing**-stage play (100+ members), and running them at seed scale
produces a leaderboard with one name on it. Recommend them when the stage gate
opens, and not before.
